Rewards Points System

Increasing checkout conversion by surfacing point value at decision time.

Role UX Designer
Timeline 3 Months
What I did Research, Ideation, Design
Tools Sketch, GA, Optimizely

Overview

When users keep coming back but ignore their rewards, something is broken. That’s the insight that launched my journey to redesign the rewards points experience for a large e-commerce platform. While loyal customers were consistently earning points, very few actually redeemed them. Even more alarming? Many weren’t even logging in—missing out on key benefits.

Business insights highlight a major challenge: low login rates and minimal redemption of earned points among B2C users, reducing the impact of the rewards strategy.

Project Objectives

  • Increase the percentage of users logged into our site
  • Boost the number of transactions paid with points

Digging Into User Behavior

To uncover why users were not redeeming their points, I began with a focused two-week research sprint aimed at deeply understanding their frustrations and barriers.

  • User Interviews: I conducted eight in-depth user interviews to explore behaviors, motivations, and the emotional friction behind the current redeem process.
  • Online Survey: I launched an online survey to capture broader patterns and validate early signals.
  • Analytics Review: I reviewed analytics data to identify critical drop-off points across the redemption flow.
  • Competitive Analysis: I performed a competitive analysis to benchmark best practices in loyalty programs.
  • User Feedback Analysis: I analyzed user comments and recordings from Hotjar, which revealed real-time pain points and moments of confusion during checkout.

Findings

After synthesizing insights from interviews, surveys, analytics, and behavioral data, several clear patterns emerged that revealed why users were not redeeming their points. These findings highlighted not only usability gaps in the redemption flow, but also deeper issues related to clarity, motivation, and trust. Below are the key insights that shaped the direction of the redesign and informed the opportunities for improvement.

Lack of Personalization

The interface does not adapt to logged-in users, missing opportunities to create tailored experiences and highlight user-specific benefits.

Flow Interruptions

Users are pulled out of the purchasing flow when prompted to visit their profile or create an account, disrupting the buying experience.

Absence of Visual Identity

There is no clear branding for the rewards program within the interface, which could help reinforce its value to users.

Hidden and Unclear Information

Users face unnecessary friction to access points cash value or understand the advantages of having an account, reducing engagement.

Missed Value Communication

Exclusive rates and rewards are not effectively presented as member benefits, limiting perceived value for both logged-in and non-logged-in users.

Hypothesis & Testing: Designing for Clarity and Motivation

With a clear understanding of the core problems, the next step was to translate these insights into actionable hypotheses that could guide experimentation. I developed a structured validation strategy focused on testing assumptions, measuring impact, and iterating quickly to move toward our goals. By formulating targeted hypotheses and running controlled tests, we were able to evaluate which changes truly improved the redemption experience and which required refinement. This approach ensured that every decision was grounded in evidence, helping us incrementally optimize the flow and drive meaningful increases in points usage.

Experiment 1 - Header

I started by testing the hypothesis that if we highlighted the benefits of being logged in, users would be more likely to log in or create an account.

Hypothesis

If we highlight the benefits of being logged in to the site, then users will be more likely to log in or create an account to access those benefits.

Elevating the Checkout Experience

Since checkout is the user’s final and most critical step, it was essential to present information in a clear, transparent way—especially when it came to how their points were being applied. My focus was ensuring users could immediately understand the value they were getting, avoid confusion, and feel confident completing their purchase. By streamlining this experience, I aimed to boost point redemption rates and overall conversion.

Experiment 4

I designed a variation that automatically applied logged-in users' available rewards at checkout, displaying the immediate discount in the order summary.

Hypothesis

Pre-selecting ‘Pay with points’ by default removes user friction, driving a higher volume of reward-based transactions and improving overall conversion.

Next steps

We continued to monitor user behavior and implemented additional actions in the next release to enhance the experience for our logged-in users and increase the number of users who created accounts in our rewards program.

  • Exclusive Offers: We introduced exclusive hotel offers specifically for logged-in users to encourage account creation and engagement.
  • Redesign of the Benefits Module: We revamped the benefits module on the homepage to clearly communicate the value of our rewards program.
  • Ongoing Enhancements: We kept enhancing the user experience during the login process, ensuring it remained seamless and inviting.

These steps were part of our commitment to creating a more engaging and rewarding experience for our users.

Key Takeaways

Make the value obvious at decision time

Switching from “points” to a clear currency value reduced mental math and increased confidence—helping more users redeem rewards.

Small, targeted changes can outperform big redesigns

Focused tweaks to visibility and messaging removed friction without disrupting the checkout flow.

Build momentum with measurable experiments

Quick A/B test wins kept stakeholders aligned and made it easier to iterate toward the OKRs.
